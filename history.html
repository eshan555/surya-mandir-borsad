History of Surya Mandir Borsad

A story of divine manifestation, community devotion, and five decades of growth.

The Founding Story

◈

Surya Mandir Borsad was built in 1972 and formally registered as a public charitable trust in 1977 at Nadiad. The temple's origin is rooted in a remarkable story of divine manifestation: Lord Surya Narayan is said to have first revealed himself through a five-month-old infant who was otherwise unable to speak. In the presence of his parents in Navaran Society, Borsad, the divine presence is said to have appeared as a mark of kumkum (vermilion), inspiring the family and the local community to build a temple in the Sun God's honour.

The local community rallied behind this vision. Local residents of Borsad donated seven acres of land for the temple, with further support from other community members. The temple was designed and engineered by a local architect and engineer. The main shikhara rises roughly 121 feet, and above the entrance sits one of the temple's most recognisable images: Surya Narayan riding his chariot drawn by seven horses, flanked by a radiant sun disc.

Today, the mandir remains an active centre of worship and community life, hosting around 50 Yagyas and 37 Kathas each year, and offering free meals and lodging to visiting pilgrims, in keeping with the temple's spirit of seva. Read more about this ongoing work on the Seva & Charity page.

Milestones

◈
1971

The Divine Manifestation

Lord Surya Narayan is said to have appeared through an infant at his family's home in Navaran Society, Borsad — the event that inspired the temple's construction.

1972

Construction of the Mandir

Seven acres of land were donated by local Borsad residents. A local architect and engineer led the design and construction.

1977

Registered as a Public Trust

The temple was formally registered as a public charitable trust at Nadiad, formalising its role as a community and religious institution.

Today

A Thriving Centre of Devotion

The mandir hosts daily aartis, around 50 yagyas and 37 kathas a year, and welcomes pilgrims with free meals and lodging — rated 4.6★ by over 2,100 visitors on Google.
